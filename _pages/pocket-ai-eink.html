I wanted an AI assistant without another distracting screen.

So, I built a small, quiet E ink device. E Ink. No feed to refresh. No apps to browse. Just enough information to tell me what’s happening, and then leave me alone but with the power of an agent behind.

My earlier screenless experiment used Gemini Live and a Hermes agent to handle everyday tasks through voice using raspberry Pi. The one thing that made it limited was it was wired in and lets face it - the form factor wasn't great :).

This is the next version: a tiny wireless device I can carry in my pocket.

The question is still the same: can I keep the useful parts of technology while spending less time looking at it?

What can it do?

Press a button and talk to it. Ask a question, post a thought to X, create slides and email them to me, or find a coloring page and print it on my home printer.

Gemini Live handles the conversation. Hermes handles the tasks that need my tools and workflows. And I don’t have to stand there waiting.

Gemini Live: just talk to it

The conversational side uses Gemini Live. I can speak naturally instead of typing a prompt into an app.

In this setup, Google Search is enabled too. In the demo, I ask for Google’s latest stock price while another task is being processed. The useful part is being able to ask for current information without opening a browser.

It talks back—and I can keep talking

Listening and speaking have their own screen states. The little face changes expression, so I can tell what the device is doing at a glance.

I can also continue having a live conversation while Hermes works in the background. Asking it to make a presentation doesn’t mean I have to wait before asking my next question.

Hermes: where things actually get done

Gemini Live hands tool-based requests to my Hermes agent, which is connected to my tools, connectors, skills, and home network.

For example: “Create five slides on AI agents and send them to my email.”

Hermes researches the topic, creates the slides using my existing slide creation skills, uploads the result, and emails me the link. When I open it, the presentation follows the style I already use.

That’s the useful part for me: starting an existing workflow with my voice.

Post a thought to X

In the demo, I ask it to post a thought about AI requiring less of our attention.

It acknowledges the request and hands it to the agent. When the task finishes, I can hear the completion result, then check the published post.

Ask, then carry on

Longer tasks run asynchronously. I can keep talking to Gemini Live, or end the conversation while the agent continues working.

The live WebSocket connection doesn’t need to stay open just because a background task is still running. Keeping those separate avoids holding an unnecessary live session while waiting.

When a result arrives, a bell appears above the home character. I can press BOOT to start a conversation and hear the result.

A small visual signal is enough to tell me something is ready.

A voice request that turns into something on paper

“Look for a cute coloring page picture and print it out on my printer.”

Because Hermes is connected to my home network, it can handle finding the picture and sending it to my printer.

A little later, there’s an actual coloring page on paper. That was probably my favorite part of the demo. :)

The sleep screen actually means sleep

E Ink can retain the image without continuously powering the display. But a sleeping character alone wouldn’t tell us much about the rest of the device.

In this firmware, idle light sleep closes the microphone and speaker, turns Wi-Fi off, puts the display into sleep, and then enters ESP32 light sleep. Pressing BOOT wakes it and the device reconnects.

The firmware checks for pending agent work before entering idle sleep. While asleep, Wi-Fi is off, so it isn’t receiving new network notifications.

I haven’t measured battery life yet. But the power-saving behavior is real, rather than just a different picture on the screen.

Why E Ink?

I wanted something that could sit quietly until I needed it. No glowing display. No scrolling. No stream of unrelated notifications competing for my attention.

Gemini Live and the remote agent still need network access. But around the house, this gives me a deliberate way to reach the assistant without picking up my phone.

Could I do many of these things from a phone? Yes. But I know what happens when I pick it up for one small thing. There’s usually a second thing waiting. And a third. :)

Build your own version

The source code and complete build guide are on GitHub.

I chose E Ink because a quiet display was the point. You could use another ESP32 device, including one with an AMOLED display, and build on the same idea.

For me, the interesting question is how little interface I need before the assistant becomes useful.

AI should require less of our attention, not more.
